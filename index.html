The gap

Two kinds of tools, and the question neither answers.

Which of today's threats concern the systems we actually run? Answering that takes an analyst who reads everything and knows the estate by heart. Most teams do not have one.

Intelligence feeds

Everything, for everyone

Thousands of reports and indicators, the same for every subscriber. Deciding what matters to you is left to your team.

Attack surface scanners

Exposure without context

A list of what you expose to the internet, with no word on who is attacking that kind of system this week.

ThreatRaven

The link between the two

Current threats matched to your organization's profile, with the intelligence, the relationship drawn from it and the asset shown side by side.

How it works

Three steps, running continuously.

Gather and analyze

Threat intelligence is collected, weighted for reliability, then analyzed. The relationships between threats and what they target are derived and verified.

  • research and advisories
  • security news
  • vulnerability data

Know what you run

A scanner discovers what you expose to the internet. You declare what sits behind it: technology, infrastructure and the third parties you rely on.

  • discovered by scan
  • declared by you
  • third parties

Alert where they meet

A threat against something you use, or one that matches your profile, becomes an alert, internet-facing or not. Exposure and exploitability decide how loud it is.

  • one alert per story
  • severity with reasons
  • full evidence chain

Anatomy of an alert

Nothing to take on trust.

An alert is a claim, and a claim needs evidence. Each one carries the chain that produced it, so an analyst can check it in a minute.

  • Why youThe asset or profile element that matched, and whether the scanner found it or you declared it.
  • How we knowThe source intel, the relationship drawn from it, and the asset it landed on.
  • How sureConfidence with its reasons: how many independent signals, how reliable, how certain the asset is.

Who it is for

Security teams without a threat intelligence department.

  • Organizations that must show they follow threats relevant to them, for example under the EU's DORA regulation.
  • Managed security providers who must keep up with the threats relevant to many customers at once.
